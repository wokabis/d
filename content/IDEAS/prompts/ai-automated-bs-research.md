5 hours a week is enough time to find out if your business idea is viable. Here’s exactly how I’d spend them.

Five hours is a Saturday morning and two weeknights after everyone else is asleep. That’s the real situation for a lot of people with an idea. A job that pays the bills, people who need you, and a business you keep promising yourself you’ll start when things calm down.

Spoiler: things never calm down.

My rule is if AI can do it, I don’t spend my hours on it. My hours go to the two things it can’t do: deciding, and talking to the people who might pay me.

Here’s the breakdown.

HOUR 1: AI does the research. I read it.

I’m not scrolling Reddit for an hour. AI is.

Prompt: “I want to help [specific customer]. Find Reddit threads, Amazon reviews and Facebook group posts where these people complain about [problem area]. Give me the 10 complaints that come up most, with links to the original posts. Quote the people, don’t summarize them. Flag anyone who says they already paid for a fix that let them down.”

My job that hour is clicking through to the sources. I want evidence, not a confident guess.

HOUR 2: Make one decision and stop debating it.

This is the hardest part and it’s why so many founders are still “exploring options” a year later. I’d pick one problem and one customer, then make AI try to talk me out of it.

Prompt: “Here’s the problem I picked and who has it: [paste]. Argue against it as hard as you can. Who already solves this? Why would anyone switch to me? What would have to be true for this to fail in 30 days? Then tell me the cheapest way to find out if I’m wrong.”

If my reasoning survives that, I’m done arguing with myself for the week. When you only have five hours, indecision is costing you time and money.

HOUR 3: Write the offer and the message.

No website or logo. One paragraph that says who it’s for, what they get and what it costs, plus the message I’d send to a real person who has the problem.

Prompt: “Using the complaints above, write a one-paragraph offer for [customer]: what they get, how fast, and the price. Then write a short personal message I could send to someone who posted one of those complaints. No hype. Under 80 words.”

HOUR 4: Talk to real people. (No prompt for this one.)

This is where AI can’t help, and it’s the most important step. Send the messages yourself. Answer the replies yourself. Pay attention to what people say when the price comes up.

Somebody telling you it’s a cool idea is worth nothing. Somebody asking how to pay means you’re on to something.

HOUR 5: Let the results write next week’s plan.

Prompt: “Here’s every reply I got this week, word for word, plus who ignored me: [paste]. Which objections came up more than once? What did people say they’d pay for? Based only on this, what’s the one thing I should change next week: the customer, the offer, the price, or where I’m finding people?”

If people bought, next week is about delivering and doing it again. If they were interested but didn’t pay, the offer or the price is off. If nobody answered, you’re in the wrong place or saying the wrong thing.

You won’t know everything after one week, but you willl know whether anyone cares, which is more than a year of thinking about it ever told you.
