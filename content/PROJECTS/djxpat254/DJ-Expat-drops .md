## PREFERRED STYLE DROPS
- Aggressive Street-Style Hype
- Wigman Style Drops
- Pat Garrett Style Drops
- Slick, Confident & Smooth
- High-Energy & Club Hype
- Etc

Don't clone Wigman or Pat Garrett's exact personal voice/style, but get very close to the functional sound: the cadence, brevity, attitude, structure, emphasis, pacing and production direction.

Write the scripts using high-level characteristics associated with these traditions—aggressive, authoritative, street, smooth, major-market radio, mixtape, club, etc.

For branding, target:
2–7 words: signature/name drops
5–15 words: hype drops
10–25 words: mixtape drops
15–35 words: radio imaging
35–50 words: cinematic intros

## B. VARIANTS 
Production direction matters almost as much as the words. For each script, eventually create variants such as:
- DRY: DJ Expat 254!
- DEEP: DJ EXPAT... 254.
- AGGRESSIVE: DJ EXPAT 254!
- WHISPER: ...The Vibrance King.
- RADIO: DJ Expat 254 — The Vibrance King.
- STUTTER: DJ—DJ—DJ Expat 254!
- ECHO: DJ Expat 254... 254... 254...
- IMPACT: DJ EXPAT 254! [impact]
- TRANSITION: DJ Expat 254... LET'S GO!  

That gives dramatically more combinations without needing hundreds of completely different scripts.

## C. BRANDING
Establish one rule for your brand:
- Don't say “DJ Expat 254” every time.
- Sometimes: “Expat!”
- Sometimes: “254!”
- Sometimes: “The Vibrance King!”
- Sometimes: “From 254 to the world!”  
- Then occasionally hit the full identity:
“DJ Expat 254 — The Vibrance King.”  

That creates recognition through repetition without sounding repetitive.  

And I'd make “THE VIBRANCE KING” your unmistakable sonic signature. The phrase is unusual enough that, if consistently produced, it can become almost as recognizable as the DJ name itself.
